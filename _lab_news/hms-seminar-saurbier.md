On Friday, August 28, 2026, the XRAI Lab hosted [Simon Saurbier](https://www.linkedin.com/in/simon-saurbier-874241286/) for an ISE
seminar on human–machine symbiosis. Simon heads the Human–Machine Systems
Research Group at IPEK – Institute of Product Engineering at the
[Karlsruhe Institute of Technology](https://www.kit.edu/english/) in Germany,
where he also leads the Human–Machine Symbiosis Laboratory.

## The talk

Simon walked us through **effect-equivalent human representation** — an approach
to representing a person inside a test setup so that the interaction between
human and machine can be *reproduced* rather than observed once. He then
connected that idea to two lines of work in his group: **smart exoskeletons**
that sense and adapt to the wearer, and **human modeling**, positioned within
the broader agenda of designing human–machine systems that are genuinely
user-centered.

## The discussion

The conversation ran well past the methods themselves. Two threads dominated:
how you validate a representation of this kind against real users, and where the
balance between humans and robotics is headed — what stays with the person, what
shifts to the machine, and who decides.

The room was full, with faculty and students from human factors, robotics,
wearable and assistive systems, and product design. Thanks to Simon for making
the trip, and to everyone who came out on a Friday morning.
