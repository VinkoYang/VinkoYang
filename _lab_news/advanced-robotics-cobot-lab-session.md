On Monday, September 28, 2026, students in **INEN 6301: Advanced Robotics**
held their lab session in the renovated XRAI Lab. They worked through the
**UR10e Operations and Motion** hands-on module and finished the session with
a group competition.

## The challenge

Each group programmed the UR10e with a **Robotiq two-finger gripper** to run
the same task as fast as possible:

1. Pick up the white cube.
2. Carry it along a fixed route made of three straight segments and one
   semicircular arc.
3. Place it at the target.

Groups were ranked by execution time, with errors recorded for each run.

## The winners

**Group 1** took first place with a run of **11.328 seconds and zero errors**.
Pictured in front of the robot, from left to right: **Nhat Ton**, **Jyothi
Supriya Chollangi**, **Ayesha Khatun**, and **Paniz Bioucki**.

## About the course

INEN 6301 is a doctoral-level course that pairs robotics theory with hands-on
skill in **collaborative robot operation and programming**. Lectures cover
kinematics, the Jacobian, dynamics and force control, and vision-guided
manipulation. The lab sessions put the same ideas to work on a real UR10e.
Students who complete the lab sessions earn the **Educational Robotics
Training – Core** certification issued by Universal Robots.

## The renovated XRAI Lab

This semester the renovated XRAI Lab is in regular use. The room holds
**30+ students** for robotics lab sessions and doubles as research space for
lab members. A multi-camera system and a lighting system are installed for
recording sessions and experiments.

The robot cell is built around a fully equipped **Universal Robots UR10e**, a
six-axis collaborative arm with a 12.5 kg payload, 1300 mm reach, ±0.05 mm
pose repeatability, and a force/torque sensor built into the tool flange.
Around it we have:

- **WINGMAN Cobot Tool Changer** by TripleA Robotics
- **Screwdriving system** with a screw feeder
- **Robotiq EPick** vacuum gripper
- **Robotiq two-finger gripper**
- **Conveyor**
- Two **SICK nanoScan3 Core** safety laser scanners
- Multiple presence sensors

With this setup we can rebuild the kinds of multi-tool robot cells found in
manufacturing and other industries: tool changes, fastening, material flow,
and safety zones that react when people step in.
