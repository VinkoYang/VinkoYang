On Friday, May 1, 2026, the Office of Undergraduate Research held the first
**AI & Software Pitch Competition** as part of the Lamar EXPO conference, in the
Neches Room of the Setzer Student Center. **Aiden Ma**, a high school researcher
from Westbrook High School working in the XRAI Lab under my mentorship, won
**First Place** with **MOTION — Musculoskeletal Optimization and Treatment
through Immersive Orthopedic Navigation**.

## The competition

The competition was built around AI-supported software with real commercial
potential, not classroom demos: entrants had to show how their solution
addressed a real-world problem and what its market value would be. Twenty-nine
students entered, and ten finalists from a range of disciplines were selected to
pitch live.

| Place | Winner(s) | Mentor | Topic |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| First | Aiden Ma (Westbrook High School) | Wenhao Yang, Industrial and Systems Engineering | MOTION (Musculoskeletal Optimization and Treatment through Immersive Orthopedic Navigation) |
| Second | Vivian Latona and Aminat Ayankoya | James Curry, Industrial and Systems Engineering | Adsight360 |
| Third | Farha Kamal and Mahesh Polineni | Rakibul Islam, Computer Science | Intelligent Degree Direction |

## The project

MOTION is an AI-driven AR rehabilitation system. It captures a patient's
movement in real time, compares it against the intended biomechanics of the
exercise, and feeds corrections back through an augmented reality interface
while the person is still moving — rather than after the fact, in a clinician's
notes. Body tracking supplies the measurement, gamified interaction keeps
patients doing the repetitions, and a remote view lets a clinician follow
progress between in-person visits. Rehabilitation is a good fit for the
competition's commercial framing, because the exercises that matter most are the
ones a patient does at home, without a therapist in the room to correct them.

The work ran from February to March 2026 in the XRAI Lab. The research write-up
lives on the [Research page](/research/), and the conference
poster is available
[here](/files/posters/Motion_Spring_Conference_Poster.pdf).

## Why a high school researcher

Aiden spent two months in the lab and came out with a working system and a pitch
that held its own against a field of university students. That is the argument
for taking high school researchers seriously: given a real problem, real
hardware, and the expectation that the result has to work in front of an
audience, they build real things. Congratulations, Aiden.
